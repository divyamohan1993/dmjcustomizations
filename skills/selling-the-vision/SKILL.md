# Selling the Vision

One second before they leave. sell the transformation, not the features. they forget what it does, they remember how it made them feel. (Jobs.)

## Gate 0: the one-second hook

First line or frame lands the single most compelling promise, alone. needs a second sentence -> failed. test: stranger reads headline + hero visual only, knows why they want it.

## The arc (keynote shape)

1. **problem they FEEL** (their words, their pain) -> 2. **agitate** (cost of status quo) -> 3. **reveal** product as resolution -> 4. **proof** (real numbers, live demo, social proof) -> 5. **one** clear next step. demo the transformation in the first frame, never open on architecture or a feature list.

## Non-negotiables

| Bar | Floor |
|---|---|
| One message | One reason to care. every competing idea cut or moved below the fold |
| One CTA | Exactly one next action. two choices = zero conversions |
| Show, not tell | Hero = working demo or result, never a paragraph |
| Tagline | A promise in 7 words or fewer. concrete beats clever; a deadline is not a reason to ship the pun |
| Honest | Real metrics, outcomes the product actually delivers. a claim it cannot cash kills trust permanently (dmj:verification-before-completion) |
| Channel fit | Same arc, sized per channel: landing hero, one pitch slide, post (hook in line 1 + live URL + one real metric), README hero. one length pasted everywhere fails the bar |

## Parallel pattern

Competing hooks or taglines: 3 to 5 parallel spikes, fresh-context "would this stop the scroll?" judge picks (dmj:dispatching-parallel-teams). marketing copy in review gets the experience lens (dmj:requesting-code-review).

**Headless:** produce the artifact; PARK brand voice and identity calls for the user.
